Where Lake Minnewashta fits in Chanhassen

.

Lake Minnewashta is the largest lake the city lists, at 680 acres and 70 feet at its deepest. Lake Minnewashta Regional Park, a 340-acre park along Highway 41, sits between Highways 5 and 7. The park has a swimming beach, boat ramp, fishing access and trails. A lake that large attracts homes of many ages and sizes, so the first thing to know is the age and layout of your own house.

The lake has its own story. A Chanhassen Historical Society newsletter says Leach's Resort once operated on the lake and that, during the 1960s fight over annexing township land, the lake community felt closer to Excelsior than to Chanhassen. The merger forming the city was approved on May 2, 1967. That history still shows up today when people give an Excelsior mailing address for a Chanhassen home.

The 55331 ZIP code and why it matters

.

Listings for streets around the lake, such as White Oak Lane, Stratford Lane and Minnewashta Court, show Chanhassen addresses with the 55331 ZIP. That ZIP is also used for Excelsior and Shorewood. When you call, give your street and say Chanhassen, because the ZIP alone may point to a different city. It also changes who you ask about utilities and permits.

Water and heaters on the lake

.

If your home is on Chanhassen's city system, the water comes from 11 municipal wells and two treatment plants. Treatment removes nearly all the iron and manganese but not hardness, which the city measures at 21 grains per gallon and suggests softening at home. Scale shortens the life of water heaters, so a heater that is slow to recover or noisy is worth a look. See water heater repair for common causes.

A private well is different, since the city's water report does not cover it. Well owners can test their own water and ask a plumber about filtration if they see staining or odor. Nobody should assume a lake address means either system without checking.

Seasonal homes and spring checks

.

Lake properties sometimes sit empty for part of the year, and unheated rooms can freeze in a Minnesota winter. A short checklist each spring catches problems before the summer rush.

  • Open each outdoor spigot and look for leaks inside the wall on the cold side.
  • Check under every sink and around the water heater for dampness.
  • Test the sump pump if you have one, and inspect the discharge line.
  • Run every drain for a minute and watch for slow spots.

A slow drain that returns after plunging may point to a deeper blockage. Drain cleaning is the usual next step, and a camera look can tell you if the line itself is damaged.

Licenses, permits and 811

.

Check any plumber's name on the Department of Labor and Industry license lookup, since the state limits the trade to licensed and registered workers. Confirm your address falls under Chanhassen before asking about permits, then contact the city's building inspections division. Digging near a lakeshore lot calls for an 811 locate request first.
