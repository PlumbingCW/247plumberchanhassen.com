The two lakes and the preserve between them

.

Lake Ann covers 119 acres and reaches 40 feet deep, according to the city. Lake Lucy covers 88 acres, reaches 20 feet and has no public boat access, though small craft can be carried in through the channel that joins the two lakes. Lake Ann Park, a community park on Lake Ann, has a boat ramp, swimming beach, trails, picnic areas and a fishing dock. Knowing which lake you live near helps a plumber picture your lot.

Between the lakes lies the Lake Ann Park Preserve. The city is carrying out a fifty-year plan to protect its forests and wetlands, following extensive negotiations with the homebuilder Lennar, and a 2018 concept plan guides the work. The preserve is parkland, so it will stay wooded. Trees and wetlands close to a property are a reason to think about roots and drainage.

Listings place homes on Lake Lucy Road in the 55317 ZIP code. The city has not published a neighborhood boundary here, so this page uses the park and the road as anchors. The main Chanhassen page covers the rest of the city, and Carver Beach on Lotus Lake has its own page.

Wooded lots and sewer lines

.

Mature trees can send roots toward pipes that carry water and moisture, and older clay or joint-heavy lines are easier for roots to enter than modern solid pipe. That does not mean your line has roots. A camera inspection shows the inside of the pipe in a few minutes and tells you whether a clog is a one-time event or a repeating problem.

Hard water, heaters and sump pumps

.

If your home is on city water, it comes from 11 wells and two treatment plants. The city treats iron and manganese but does not soften, and hardness comes in at 21 grains per gallon. Homeowners are encouraged to soften at home, since scale gathers on fixtures and inside heaters. A heater showing its age is a good candidate for replacement before it fails.

Homes near lakes and wetlands often have a sump pit, though nothing found here shows these homes flood more often. A working pump is a simple safeguard. Test it with a few gallons, check the float and make sure the discharge line leads away from the house. See sump pump repair if it fails the test.

A quick checklist for homes near the lakes

.
  • Know where your main shutoff is and test that it turns.
  • Note any trees close to the sewer line path from house to street.
  • Pour water into the sump pit and listen for the pump to start.
  • Write down the age of the water heater from its label.

Licenses, permits and 811

.

Minnesota allows plumbing only by licensed or registered people, and the state's online lookup lets you verify a name before the truck arrives. For sewer line work, ask the Chanhassen building inspections division what a repair or replacement needs. Tree roots and buried lines share the same ground, so call 811 before any trenching.
