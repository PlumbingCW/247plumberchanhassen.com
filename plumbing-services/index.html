How we decide between repair and replacement

.

A good plumber does not push the biggest job. For most fixtures and equipment, the question is whether the repair will hold and what it costs compared with the replacement. A ten-year-old tank water heater with one failed element is a repair. A fifteen-year-old tank leaking at the seam is a replacement, and no amount of parts will change that.

The same logic applies to pipes. One pinhole in a copper line is a repair. Pinholes in three rooms in two years suggest the whole run is failing, and a repiping quote starts to look reasonable. Ask the plumber to explain why, and ask what happens if you wait.

Repair or replace: quick guide
SituationUsually better as a repairUsually better as a replacement
Water heater.Single failed part, tank sound.Leaking tank, heavy scale, old unit.
Drain line.One clog, pipe in good shape.Repeated clogs from a cracked or sagging pipe.
Supply pipe.One isolated leak.Pinholes or corrosion in several places.
Toilet.Worn flapper or fill valve.Cracked bowl or repeated seal failure.
Sump pump.Stuck float or clogged intake.Failed motor, old pump, frequent cycling.

Licensed work and clear pricing

.

State law limits plumbing work to licensed or registered people and to staff of a licensed contractor. Any license can be verified through the labor department's online lookup. Jobs are done to the state plumbing code, chapter 4714 of Minnesota Rules.

Code knowledge is part of what you pay for. The Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) is the model Minnesota started from, and it shapes everyday details such as traps, vents, relief valves and backflow protection. Your plumber explains which details touch your job.

Every job begins with a diagnosis and a stated price. A same-day slot is offered if the schedule has room. For anything beyond a like-for-like swap, the Chanhassen building inspections division can say whether a permit applies.
