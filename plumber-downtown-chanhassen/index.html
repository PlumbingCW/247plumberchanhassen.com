Where downtown Chanhassen sits

.

Downtown Chanhassen runs along West 78th Street, the part of the city where apartments, restaurants and shops sit close together. The city's history page says growth stayed slow until the late 1960s, when the first mini-mall opened at Great Plains Boulevard and West 78th Street. Chanhassen Dinner Theatres opened at 501 West 78th Street in October 1968. Buildings put up decades apart rarely share the same plumbing, so the building and its age matter more than the street name.

Newer housing has joined the corridor recently. Venue on 78th, at 541 West 78th Street, has 134 apartments and began filling in fall 2019 after a July 2018 groundbreaking, according to the Chanhassen Villager. In April 2024 the City Council approved a redevelopment near West 78th Street and Market Boulevard with about 310 apartments over ground-floor commercial space. More households in one building means more shared plumbing, and shared plumbing is where a small leak becomes a neighbor's problem.

This is a mixed commercial and residential district, not a street of single-family homes. A caller here might be a tenant, a condo owner or a shop manager. The newer neighborhood of Avienda near Highway 212 has its own page, and the main Chanhassen page covers the rest of the city.

Water and drains in the downtown core

.

The city draws drinking water from groundwater through 11 municipal wells, 441 to 530 feet deep. Treatment removes nearly all the iron and manganese but does not soften the water. Hardness is listed at 21 grains per gallon, and the city points residents toward softeners. Scale builds inside water heaters, so a heater that is slow to heat may be fighting scale. Water heater repair often starts with that question.

The city's 2023 water report found lead under 2 parts per billion and copper at 0.85 parts per million, both under federal action levels, from 30 customer taps sampled in August 2022. Residents can ask the city whether a service line is lead. Ask if you own an older building, because the answer sits in city records, not in the plumbing under your sink.

Restaurants and cafes put grease and food solids into their drains, which clog differently than hair and soap. The right fix depends on whether the blockage sits in a branch, a floor drain or the building's main line. For a home or apartment, drain cleaning usually starts at the nearest cleanout. For a shop the work may fall under commercial plumbing, so say so when you call.

  • Ask for the cause of the problem in writing if the building or your insurer needs it.

Permits, licenses and 811

.

Anyone who opens walls or replaces fixtures in a downtown building should hold a Minnesota license or work under someone who does. The Department of Labor and Industry's Check a License page lets you confirm a name in a minute. For permits, the Chanhassen building inspections division is the office to ask, and a building manager may already know what a project needs. Call 811 before any digging near the building.
