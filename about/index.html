How we work: by phone, not by form

.

There are no web forms on this site and no chat widgets collecting your details. You call the 24/7 line, talk to a person and describe the problem. That is faster in an emergency, and it means your address and phone number only go where you intended.

For non-urgent questions, you can write to contact@247plumberchanhassen.com. Email is read during normal working hours, so use the phone for anything involving water on the floor, a smell of gas or sewage in the house.

Licensing in Minnesota, and how to check it yourself

.

State rules say plumbing work belongs to licensed or registered people, a licensed contractor's employees or workers under supervision. The Department of Labor and Industry sorts licenses into three classes: journeyworker, master and contractor.

  1. Ask the plumber for a license type and number before work begins.
  2. Go to the state labor department's site and open its license and registration lookup.
  3. Search by name or number and confirm that the license is active and the name matches.
  4. If anything does not match, stop and ask. A legitimate plumber will expect the question.

Do the same for any other plumber you hire, including competitors. Verifying a license takes two minutes and protects you from unlicensed work that can void insurance claims or fail inspection.

What we do and what we do not do

.

We do

Repair and replace water heaters, clear drains and sewers, find and fix leaks, handle burst pipes, install fixtures and service sump pumps and boilers.

We do

Explain the cause of a problem, the options for fixing it and the price, before any work starts.

We do not

Take on foundation structure fixes, electrical wiring, well pump swaps or anything beyond a plumber's licensed scope.

We do not

Quote a firm price without seeing the problem, promise an arrival time or claim a result that cannot be known in advance.

When another trade is the right answer, the dispatcher says so. A cracked foundation wall needs a structural contractor, a dead outlet needs an electrician and a faulty gas line may need the utility. Sending the wrong tradesperson is a waste of your night.

When to call right away, and what to do first

.
  • Water is running and you cannot stop it: close the main shutoff, then call the 24/7 line.
  • Sewage is backing up through a floor drain or tub: stop using every fixture in the house and call.
  • You smell gas: leave the building, call the gas utility or 911, and call a plumber only after you are outside and safe.
  • Water is near outlets, a panel or a sump pump cord: stay clear, and cut power at the breaker only if you can reach it dry.
  • Hot water has failed in freezing weather: call early, because exposed pipes can split before the house warms back up.

How pricing works

.

Each job is priced after the plumber sees it. The price depends on access, the size and age of the equipment, the materials needed, the time of day and whether other work is needed once the pipe is opened. The price is stated up front, and the decision to proceed is yours.

Insurance is a separate conversation. Some water damage is covered by homeowners policies and some is not, and the plumber cannot decide that for your carrier. Photograph everything, keep damaged parts if you can and ask your insurer what documentation they need.

Why a dispatch line instead of a contact form

.

Most plumbing emergencies are time-sensitive in a way that a form cannot handle. A form cannot ask whether the water is off, hear the sound of a running pipe or tell you to stop flushing. A person on the phone can do all three in the first thirty seconds, and then send help.

The tradeoff is that you have to make a call. We think that is the right trade, and it keeps your details out of inboxes and databases you never agreed to join.

Local standards we work to

.

Jobs are held to the 2020 Minnesota Plumbing Code (chapter 4714 of Minnesota Rules), effective December 17, 2021, which adapts the 2018 Uniform Plumbing Code for the state. Permits are a question for the Chanhassen building inspections division. Dial 811, the Gopher State One Call line, ahead of any digging.
