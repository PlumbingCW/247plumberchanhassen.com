Bathroom Sink Not Draining: What to Check, in Order

.

By the Chanhassen 24/7 Plumbing team. Published 2026-10-16. Updated 2026-10-16.

A bathroom sink not draining in Chanhassen is rarely a mystery. Most cases come down to a stopper, a hair mat or a trap, and this guide walks through each in the order a plumber would.

.

Start with the stopper, not the pipe

.

A bathroom sink not draining usually has a simple cause close to the surface. In Chanhassen homes, the pop-up stopper is the first place to look. Hair wraps around the pivot rod and the stopper's base, and soap scum glues it all into a mat.

Lift out the stopper if your model allows it. Use a flashlight to look down into the drain opening, and clean off any dark slime with an old toothbrush. Then rinse and run water again. This one step solves a surprising share of slow sinks.

Hair, soap and the sticky mat that forms

.

Bathroom drains rarely face grease. They face hair, toothpaste, shaving residue and soap. These combine into a gray rope that clings to the pipe wall and narrows the opening a little more each week. A plastic hair-catching strip, sold at hardware stores, can pull a lot of it out from above.

Use the strip gently and keep it flat so the barbs do not snag on the pipe. Pull it up slowly, wipe off what comes with it and repeat until nothing more arrives. If the strip finds nothing and the sink is still slow, the blockage is deeper.

Cleaning the P-trap

.

The curved pipe under the sink holds a small plug of water that keeps sewer gas out of the house. It is also where heavy debris settles. Set a bucket underneath, loosen the two slip nuts by hand or with pliers and slide the trap free.

Empty the trap into the bucket and rinse it in another sink or outdoors. Check the rubber washers before reassembling. Put the slip nuts on in the right order, hand-tighten and then run water to look for leaks. A trap that fits badly will drip for weeks before you notice.

Adjusting the lift rod

.

Sometimes the sink drains fine when the stopper is out but holds water badly or drains badly when it is in. The culprit is the pivot rod, which sets how far the stopper lifts. If the rod is set too low, the stopper opens only a hair, and water slips past slowly.

Under the sink, loosen the clip that holds the rod to the lever strap, slide the strap to a higher hole and tighten again. Test the lift from above. A little lift, about the width of a pencil, is usually plenty, and it may be the whole fix for a stubborn sink.

Why Chanhassen drains scale up and clog faster

.

Hardness in the city supply is 21 grains per gallon, and treatment takes out iron and manganese without softening it. Soap reacts with those minerals to form a sticky film that coats the inside of a bathroom drain and holds hair like glue. That is why drains in unsoftened homes often clog sooner than the same drain in a house with a softener.

Eden Prairie softens its municipal water to about 5 grains per gallon, and Chaska's report lists 26. If you moved between towns, expect a different cleaning rhythm. In Chanhassen, a monthly hot-water rinse of the stopper and a yearly look at the trap are cheap habits. A softener or filtration system is the long-term fix for the film.

When the vent is the culprit

.

Every drain needs air. A vent pipe through the roof lets air enter behind the water so it flows rather than clings. Leaves, nests or frost can block the top of a vent, and that produces gurgles and slow draining that a trap cleaning will not touch.

Vents are part of the code for a reason. Minnesota's plumbing code descends from the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC). That model generally expects each trap to be vented so water does not siphon out or drain sluggishly, and a plumber can confirm what applies before anyone opens a wall.

You can tell by sound. If the sink glugs, sucks air or drains in surges, and toilets nearby burp when it empties, suspect a vent. Winter frost on a small vent pipe can close it entirely, which makes the problem seasonal. Do not climb onto an icy roof to look. A plumber can test and clear a vent safely.

Telling a sink clog from a main line problem

.

Run water in the tub and flush a toilet, then watch the sink. If the sink bubbles or rises, or a floor drain in the basement burps, the blockage is shared. That calls for professional drain cleaning on the branch or main line, since a plunger and a trap wrench do not reach far enough.

Stop running water if more than one fixture is affected. Every flush or shower adds to the backup, and a basement floor drain is where it will surface. Our dispatchers can help sort out whether it is urgent while you wait.

What a plumber brings that a home kit does not

.

A small drain machine, called a cable or snake, reaches past the trap and into the wall. A camera can show the pipe wall and the exact spot of a stubborn blockage. For repeated problems, a plumber may recommend jetting or a pipe inspection rather than another round of cleaning.

If the sink drains but the faucet drips, sprays or sputters, that is a different job, and faucet repair is the right service. Dispatch in Chanhassen also sends crews to Chaska and Eden Prairie, and the order of checks above holds for a bathroom in either town.

What not to do

.
  • Do not stack several chemical openers one after another.
  • Do not use a plunger on a sink with a stopper in place and an open overflow without blocking the overflow with a wet rag.
  • Do not force a cable into a pop-up assembly without removing the stopper.
  • Do not leave a trap disassembled and run water.
  • Do not ignore gurgling in more than one fixture.

Questions about a bathroom sink that will not drain

.

A partial clog is the usual reason. Hair, soap residue and toothpaste build a lining on the pipe wall and on the stopper assembly, and water squeezes past until the blockage grows. Cleaning the stopper and the trap usually restores full flow.

Look under the sink for a horizontal rod held by a clip or nut behind the drain pipe. Release it, then lift the stopper straight out from above. Some models twist out, and some lift off with a gentle tug.
