Hard water at 26 grains is hard on a tank heater

.

Chaska pulls its drinking water from five wells, 333 to 817 feet deep. The city's report lists the hardness at 26 grains per gallon, a step above Chanhassen's 21. The same report calls a water softener very important for improving the water's aesthetics.

Here is what that means in your basement. Heat drives dissolved minerals out of the water, and they settle as scale on the bottom of a tank and on heating elements. A heater that groans, pops or runs out of hot water early is often telling you it is carrying a lot of it.

Flushing the tank can help if it is done early. Once the scale has hardened, a replacement is often the cheaper long-term move, and a plumber can show you the sediment that drains out. If you are weighing it, replacing the heater usually makes more sense than a string of repairs on a tank that is well into its life.

Low ground, river valley storms and the sump pit

.

Downtown Chaska sits on the Minnesota River, and parts of the valley sit low. In wet spells, groundwater and storm runoff push toward basements in low spots. A sump pump is the machine between you and a wet floor, and it tends to fail on exactly the day it is needed.

Pour a bucket of water into the pit this week. The float should lift and the pump should run and empty the pit. If it hums without moving water, or does not start, get it looked at before the storm, not during it. Our sump pump repair page lists the usual causes.

A slow drain that gurgles when the washer runs is another warning sign, especially in older lines. A professional drain cleaning clears the blockage and shows whether the pipe itself is the problem.
