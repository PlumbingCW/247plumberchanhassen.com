What Avienda is

.

Avienda is a planned development in Chanhassen near Highway 212 and Powers Boulevard. The Chanhassen Villager reported in August 2020 that it covers 118 acres, and a concept plan was approved in November 2016. The plan divides the site into a village center, a mixed-use area, multi-family housing and low-density residential with small-lot homes. Because the pieces were built in phases, two neighbors a block apart can live in very different buildings.

The city's project page lists Avienda rowhomes and Avienda townhomes. A developer tracking site reported plans for a four-story apartment building at the southwest corner of Highway 212 and Powers Boulevard, still at the early open-house stage when reported. Builder marketing also mentions low-maintenance villas. Check what stands at your address today, since plans change and not every proposal gets built.

Plumbing in newer attached housing

.

Newer homes shift the first question from how old the pipes are to who is responsible for them. In a townhome or rowhome, an association may own the exterior and shared lines while you own what sits inside your unit. Read the documents or ask the manager, because a plumber hired for the wrong pipe can leave you with a bill that was never yours.

None of this means newer plumbing is likely to fail. It means documents, shutoff locations and responsibility matter more than a pipe's age. Water heater installation and toilet repair are everyday jobs in homes of any age, and a plumber can handle them once you know who authorizes the work.

City water in a newer development

.

The city's own water report is the place to check what comes out of an Avienda tap. It describes untreated hardness of 21 grains per gallon and a recommendation that homes add their own softener. Scale forms on fixtures and inside water heaters at any building age, so a newer unit is not exempt. See water softeners and filtration for the options.

Lead and copper results in that 2023 report both sat under federal action levels, but they come from 30 customer taps sampled in August 2022. Treat them as a snapshot of the system, not a test of your own tap. If you want your own result, ask the city or an accredited lab.

What to do when a newer building has a leak

.
  • Shut the fixture valve or the unit shutoff, then confirm the water has stopped.
  • Report the leak in writing to the manager or association.
  • Photograph the source and any damage with the date visible.
  • Hold on to old parts if the builder or insurer may want to see them.

Licenses, permits and 811

.

A newer townhome still follows the same state rules: licensed or registered people only, and the Department of Labor and Industry lets you search a plumber by name. Ask your association whether it requires its own approved vendors or notice before work. The Chanhassen building inspections division answers permit questions, and 811 covers any digging.
