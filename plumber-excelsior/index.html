Victorian-era houses and what is behind the walls

.

Excelsior has many Victorian-era houses, and the Water Street commercial district is on the National Register of Historic Places. Behind that charm, older homes may still carry original or early replacement plumbing.

Galvanized steel supply pipe rusts from the inside, narrowing the opening until a shower trickles. Cast iron and clay drain laterals, the pipes that run from the house toward the street, can crack, sag or fill with roots. Neither problem announces itself until a drain backs up.

If you own an old house and have never had the main drain scoped, a sewer inspection with a camera shows the real condition without a shovel. Do it before you buy, before you remodel or after the second backup.

High water, sump pumps and the lake

.

A town on a lake lives close to the water table. Basements near the shore can get damp after a wet week, and the sump pump is what keeps them dry. Ask yourself when you last tested yours.

Pump failures cluster on stormy nights, which is why emergency calls come in then. A backup power source or battery option is worth asking about. A burst supply line while the sump is running is the double emergency to avoid, so keep the main valve accessible.

Seasonal homes and winter shutdowns

.

Some Excelsior properties are used more in summer, with docks, lake cabins and guest cottages. A house left cold without being winterized is a classic cause of an emergency burst pipe. The damage is hidden until you open the door in spring.

Before closing a place for the season, shut the main, drain the lines and ask a plumber how to handle the water heater and traps. We keep that advice general because every system is different. If a pipe has already split, our burst pipe repair team can help.
