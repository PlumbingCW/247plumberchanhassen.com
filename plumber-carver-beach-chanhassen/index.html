What Carver Beach means in Chanhassen

.

Carver Beach is a name tied to the northwest shore of Lotus Lake. The city's beaches page lists Carver Beach on Lotus Lake at 6891 Lotus Trail, with a swimming beach, picnic area, playground, trail, dock and canoe rack. Lotus Lake covers 240 acres and reaches 29 feet at its deepest, according to the city. If you live near the lake, say so on the phone, because a lakeside lot often has different drainage than one up the hill.

Some data sites use Carver Beach for the surrounding homes, and listings for Lotus Trail, Mohawk Drive and Indian Hill Road carry the 55317 ZIP code. The city has not published a boundary for the neighborhood, so this page treats the beach and Lotus Trail as anchors. It does not claim exact edges. Chanhassen's main city page covers the whole city.

Homes of different ages

.

A Chanhassen Historical Society photo credited to Edwin and Bernie Kerber shows dirt being scraped for a new house in Carver Beach in spring 1947. One data aggregator, NeighborhoodScout, reports that most homes in its Carver Beach area were built between 1970 and 1999, with some from 1940 to 1969, and that nearly all are detached single-family houses. Those figures are a census-based estimate, so treat them as a guide.

The practical point is that one block can hold original fixtures next to updated ones.

Age alone tells you little about the pipes. Many older homes have had supply lines, water heaters and drains replaced over the years, and plenty of newer homes have problems of their own. A plumber should look at what is actually installed, such as the pipe material at the meter and under the sink, before suggesting any work.

City water and hard water

.

The city of Chanhassen gets its drinking water from groundwater through 11 wells and treats it for iron and manganese, but it does not soften it. That puts hardness at 21 grains per gallon, a level the city suggests treating at home. Whether your own address is connected to city water is a question for the city, so ask before you assume. If it is, expect scale in water heaters and on fixtures.

Lakes, basements and sump pumps

.

Living near a lake does not mean a wet basement, and nothing found here shows that Carver Beach homes flood more than others. It does mean the grade, the soil and the water table matter more when you buy or maintain a home. A working sump pump is cheap insurance if you already have a pit. Test it each spring and after any long rain.

  • Pour a few gallons into the pit and confirm the pump starts and the water leaves.
  • Check that the discharge pipe sends water away from the foundation.
  • Keep a battery backup in mind if the power goes out during storms.
  • Photograph anything damp and note the date.

Licenses, permits and 811

.

Before hiring for lake-area work, look the plumber up on the state's Check a License page, because Minnesota restricts the trade to licensed and registered workers. Whether a job needs a permit depends on the scope, and the Chanhassen building inspections division can say. If a repair means digging near the yard or the shoreline, call 811 first so buried lines get marked.
