Which water system is your Shorewood house on?

.

Before an emergency plumber in Shorewood can help, one question has to be answered: city water or private well? Shorewood's utility serves 1,473 connections and 435 hydrants, which is far fewer than the number of homes in a city of about 7,800 people. So some houses here run on private wells.

The answer changes what happens when something fails. In a house on the municipal supply, shutting the main inside the house stops the leak at its source. A well home has a pump and a pressure tank, and those need their own shutoff steps. The 24/7 line asks this first.

Private well homes: where a plumber stops and a well contractor starts

.

A well system has a pump in the ground, a pressure tank in the house and pipes between them. The pump and casing are a separate trade. A plumber covers the house side: the tank connections, shutoff and check valves, supply piping, the water heater and every fixture.

If the pressure cycles on and off rapidly, or the taps spit air, the cause might be the pressure tank or the pump. We will diagnose what is on our side and tell you honestly when it is not. The city's page tells private-well owners to test their own water, and that is good advice, because nobody else will.

Iron staining and the level 22 softener tip

.

Iron shows up as orange or brown streaks in a toilet bowl, on laundry and around drains. Shorewood's city water carries about 0.28 ppm of iron, small but enough to stain over time. The city also suggests setting a softener to level 22 for city water, which is useful if you are dialing in a unit.

That level is not a universal setting. It is tuned to city water, so a well household should not copy it. A softener or iron filter is a plumbing install, and softener and filtration work starts with a water test. Skip the guesswork.

Sewer: city main or private line?

.

The Shorewood Public Works department runs the sanitary sewer collection system. The pipe from your house to the main is yours to maintain, though, and that is where most clogs begin. If one fixture backs up, the clog is probably local. If every drain gurgles, the blockage may be farther along.

On the private side of a sewer connection, repairs follow Minnesota's plumbing code, which rests on the model Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC). Expect inspectors to check approved pipe, sound joints and cleanout access on that stretch. Your plumber can say what applies, and Shorewood city staff can explain how inspection is handled there.

Before calling the city, it helps to know if the problem is in your line. A plumber can clear it with drain cleaning and a camera, and will tell you if the main is the issue. In that case, Public Works is the number to call.

Shorewood runs along the south shore of Lake Minnetonka, with Spray and Shady islands and part of Enchanted in its waters. Highway 7 is the main route across the city, and Mound sits across the water, and Eden Prairie is another nearby city we serve. Lake views are great, but a house close to the water often deals with damp basements.
